# Biologic Monitoring Dashboard Reference

The tables below document baseline screening, follow-up monitoring, and safety considerations for systemic dermatology treatments. Data reflect FDA prescribing information and high-quality guideline sources (captured September 19, 2025).

## Biologic Agents

| Drug / Class | Key Dermatology Uses | Baseline Screening | Ongoing Monitoring | Critical Alerts / Cautions | Key References |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| TNF inhibitors (adalimumab, infliximab, etanercept, certolizumab) | Plaque psoriasis, psoriatic arthritis, hidradenitis suppurativa | TB test; HBV serologies; consider CBC/LFTs to establish baseline | Clinical review for infection; repeat CBC/LFTs if risk factors or combination therapy | Hold for serious infection; monitor HBV carriers throughout therapy; counsel on endemic mycoses exposure | HUMIRA full prescribing information (FDA Access Data) |
| IL-17 inhibitors (secukinumab, ixekizumab, brodalumab, bimekizumab) | Psoriasis / PsA; HS (secukinumab/bimekizumab) | TB screening; assess IBD history; review vaccination status | Watch for mucocutaneous candidiasis; clinical surveillance for neutropenia/IBD flare | Caution in known or suspected IBD; avoid live vaccines during therapy | COSENTYX prescribing information (FDA Access Data) |
| IL-23 inhibitors (guselkumab, risankizumab, tildrakizumab) | Plaque psoriasis; PsA; IBD indications (guselkumab) | TB evaluation; complete age-appropriate vaccines; obtain LFTs if using IV induction in IBD | Clinical infection surveillance; liver enzymes per label when treating Crohn’s/UC | Avoid live vaccines while on treatment; counsel on infection symptoms; hepatotoxicity monitoring for IBD dosing | TREMFYA prescribing information (FDA Access Data) |
| IL-12/23 inhibitor (ustekinumab) | Plaque psoriasis; PsA; Crohn’s disease; ulcerative colitis | TB screen; review malignancy history; ensure immunizations (avoid live vaccines once started) | Clinical review for infection; periodic skin checks for NMSC in high-risk patients | Potential malignancy risk; avoid live vaccines; consider susceptibility to atypical infections | STELARA prescribing information (FDA Access Data) |
| IL-4/13 blockers (dupilumab, tralokinumab, lebrikizumab) | Atopic dermatitis, prurigo nodularis (dupilumab), asthma/COPD overlap | No routine labs; treat helminths before initiation; ensure inactivated vaccines up to date | Monitor for conjunctivitis/keratitis and eosinophilia if symptomatic; assess new joint pain | Avoid live vaccines; hold therapy if helminth infection fails standard treatment; escalate ocular symptoms early | DUPIXENT full prescribing information (Regeneron) |
| IL-31R antagonist (nemolizumab) | Prurigo nodularis; atopic dermatitis (≥12 y) | Complete age-appropriate vaccinations; avoid live vaccines once on drug | Clinical monitoring for hypersensitivity; no mandated routine labs | Hypersensitivity reactions; avoid live vaccines during therapy | NEMLUVIO prescribing information (FDA Access Data) |
| Anti-IgE (omalizumab) | Chronic spontaneous urticaria; IgE-mediated food allergy adjunct | Observe initial doses in clinic; baseline IgE optional | Post-injection observation; reinforce anaphylaxis plan | Boxed warning for anaphylaxis—ensure emergency management plan | XOLAIR prescribing information (FDA Access Data) |
| Anifrolumab | Cutaneous lupus (off-label) / systemic lupus erythematosus | TB and HBV screening; review zoster vaccination | Monitor for infusion reactions and recurrent infections; consider zoster prophylaxis | Elevated herpesvirus reactivation risk—counsel on zoster signs | Anifrolumab safety reviews (PubMed 39245905) |

## Targeted and Conventional Systemics

| Agent / Class | Dermatology Indications | Baseline Labs / Assessments | Ongoing Labs / Exams | Special Precautions | Key References |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Upadacitinib (JAK1) | Atopic dermatitis; off-label inflammatory dermatoses | CBC, LFTs, lipid panel, TB, HBV/HCV; pregnancy test when relevant | CBC/LFTs at 4–8 weeks then q3–6 months; lipids ~12 weeks; reassess cardiovascular risk | Boxed warnings for serious infection, MACE, malignancy, thrombosis; avoid live vaccines | RINVOQ prescribing information (FDA Access Data) |
| Abrocitinib (JAK1) | Atopic dermatitis | CBC (platelets/neutrophils), lipids, TB, HBV/HCV | Platelets monthly ×3 months then q3 months; CBC/LFT & lipids per risk | JAK boxed warnings; dose-adjust or hold for thrombocytopenia | CIBINQO prescribing information (FDA Access Data) |
| Baricitinib (JAK1/2) | Alopecia areata; atopic dermatitis | CBC, LFTs, lipids, TB, HBV/HCV | CBC/LFT 4–8 weeks then periodically; lipids at 12 weeks | Boxed warnings (infection, MACE, malignancy, thrombosis); evaluate DVT/PE symptoms promptly | OLUMIANT prescribing information (FDA Access Data) |
| Ritlecitinib (JAK3/TEC) | Alopecia areata | TB & HBV screen; CBC, LFTs; pregnancy testing | CBC/LFTs during first 3 months; consider lipids | JAK-class boxed warnings; avoid live vaccines | LITFULO prescribing information (FDA Access Data) |
| Deucravacitinib (TYK2) | Plaque psoriasis | TB screen; review immunization status; baseline CBC/LFT reasonable | Periodic CBC/LFT based on risk; reinforce infection vigilance | Infection and lab warnings; avoid combining with other potent immunosuppressants | SOTYKTU prescribing information (FDA Access Data) |
| Apremilast (PDE4) | Psoriasis / PsA | Weight, mood history | Monitor weight and mood; adjust if significant loss or mood change | Counsel on weight loss risk and neuropsychiatric events | OTEZLA prescribing information (FDA Access Data) |
| Methotrexate | Psoriasis (systemic), CTCL adjunct | CBC, CMP, hepatitis B/C, pregnancy test; consider chest X-ray/TB screen; start folate | CBC & CMP q2–4 weeks initially, spacing to q1–3 months once stable | Hepatotoxicity and myelosuppression monitoring; teratogenic—avoid pregnancy | AAD psoriasis guideline (2023) |
| Cyclosporine | Severe psoriasis; acute atopic dermatitis flares | BP, creatinine (×2), CBC, CMP, lipids, Mg, uric acid | BP & creatinine q2 weeks for 3 months then monthly; check electrolytes/lipids periodically | Nephrotoxicity/hypertension; limit duration to short intermittent courses | JAAD cyclosporine consensus statements |
| Mycophenolate mofetil | Autoimmune blistering disease, CTCL adjunct | CBC, CMP, pregnancy testing (two negatives), BP; review vaccinations | CBC/CMP q2 weeks ×6 weeks then q3 months once stable; annual minimum q12 weeks | Teratogenic; monitor for cytopenias and renal decline; ensure contraception | Specialist Pharmacy Service monitoring guidance |
| Azathioprine (± TPMT/NUDT15 testing) | Pemphigus, connective tissue dermatoses | CBC, CMP; TPMT/NUDT15 phenotype or genotype before start | CBC/LFT q1–2 weeks initially, spacing to q1–3 months when stable | Myelosuppression risk if TPMT/NUDT15 deficient; major interaction with allopurinol | CPIC TPMT/NUDT15 guidelines |
| Hydroxychloroquine | Cutaneous lupus, dermatomyositis, porphyria cutanea tarda | Baseline ophthalmologic exam within 1 year; weight-based dosing ≤5 mg/kg actual | Annual retinal screening after 5 years (earlier if high risk); monitor renal function | Retinopathy risk escalates with dose >5 mg/kg or prolonged duration | American Academy of Ophthalmology screening recommendations |
| Acitretin | Psoriasis, keratinization disorders | Two negative pregnancy tests, LFTs, fasting lipids; counsel on contraception & alcohol abstinence | LFTs/lipids q1–3 months; pregnancy test monthly and q3 months for 3 years post-therapy | Strict contraception for 3 years post-stop; avoid alcohol to prevent etretinate formation | SORIATANE prescribing information (FDA Access Data) |
| Isotretinoin | Nodulocystic acne, refractory follicular disorders | Pregnancy tests (CLIA setting pre-initiation), LFTs, fasting lipids | Pregnancy test monthly; labs at ~2 months then as indicated when stable | Teratogenic—iPLEDGE compliance; consider reduced lab frequency after stable results | iPLEDGE REMS (FDA, 2024 update) |
| Thalidomide | ENL, refractory aphthosis, prurigo; off-label derm use | Pregnancy testing (two negatives), contraception counseling; assess VTE risk | Pregnancy tests weekly ×1 month then q4 weeks; monitor neuropathy and VTE risk | REMS enrollment; dual contraception; consider VTE prophylaxis with steroids | THALOMID prescribing information |
| Intravenous immunoglobulin | Autoimmune blistering disease, dermatomyositis, urticaria variants | Screen IgA level; review renal function, thrombotic risk; ensure hydration | Monitor vitals during infusion; renal function & urine output in high-risk patients; hemolysis labs with repeat dosing | Aseptic meningitis, renal failure, thrombosis—use slow infusion and sucrose-free products when appropriate | IVIG adverse effect reviews (e.g., PubMed 16391392) |

*Prepared September 19, 2025. Maintain quarterly literature surveillance for label or guideline changes.*
