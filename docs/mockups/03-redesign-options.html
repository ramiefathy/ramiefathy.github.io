Redesign options for your site

What's being replaced: the current design commits hard to a reference-book conceit — stamped section labels, decorative barcodes, numbered plates, volume references in the hero and footer — plus a warm bone/terracotta palette, heavy black borders, and a two-layer WebGL shader hero. The content architecture underneath (profile, apps, publications as data) is solid and carries over unchanged.

What these options do differently: every mockup below drops the conceit entirely — plain section names, no decorative numbering systems, no metaphor in the copy. Each is a committed, distinct visual direction rendered with your real content: name, bio, the four flagship apps, and actual publications. Scroll through all six, or jump with the index. A comparison table and recommendation are at the end.

01Warm Editorial

The closest evolution of what you have: keeps the warmth and the serif literacy, sheds every gimmick. Reads like a well-set essay collection — a physician-writer's site first, a developer's second.

  • Keeps the warm paper tone and serif voice you already own, so the change feels like a refinement, not a rebrand.
  • Drops black plate borders, stamps, and mono microcopy; structure comes from whitespace and hairlines.
  • Projects become a numbered list, not cards — quieter, more editorial, easier to scan.
  • Publications set as proper hanging-indent citations.

02Swiss Grid

International-style rigor: white ground, one red accent, a visible grid where the borders are the layout. Confident and structural without needing any conceit — the discipline is the personality.

  • Oversized flush-left name; asymmetric 12-column grid with facts in the right rail.
  • Projects live in a ruled grid — the lines do the work cards usually fake.
  • Publications as a dense registry table with tabular year column; suits a growing list.
  • Scales beautifully to CV-style pages; the strictness keeps future additions tidy.
Palette
#FFFFFF white #111111 ink #C8102E red #555555 muted
Type
Space Grotesk (display + body) · Space Mono (labels, data)
Structure
Exposed grid with 1px rules; black footer band; zero shadows, zero radius
Effort
Medium — new layout skeleton, but reuses your existing mono font asset

03Dark Technical

A builder's site: near-black ground, one warm amber accent, surfaces elevated by lightness instead of shadows. Signals "I ship working software" — the strongest frame for the apps and the AI-evaluation research.

  • Dark UI puts the apps front and center — screenshots and demos pop against it.
  • A live-feeling status line ("Currently: …") replaces decorative ornament with real signal.
  • Amber, not the usual dark-mode teal/purple — warmer and more distinctive against slate.
  • Best candidate if you want dark + light themes; this becomes the dark half.

#10141A ground #1A2028 surface #E8EAED text #F5B942 amber
Type
IBM Plex Sans (body) · JetBrains Mono (labels, data)
Structure
Left-weighted hero; surface-elevation cards; hairline dividers; 6–8px radii
Effort
Medium–high — full token flip plus contrast re-audit of every page

04Calm Product

A contemporary product-site register: soft cool neutrals, white cards, one teal accent, generous radii used deliberately. The most approachable of the six — reads instantly to patients, program directors, and collaborators alike.

  • Hero pairs the intro with a stat block (publications, apps, stage of training) — concrete numbers instead of decoration.
  • Radius is a scale, not a constant: 14px cards, 10px buttons, pills for tags only.
  • The friendliest surface for app screenshots and future case-study pages.
  • Lowest-risk direction; the trade-off is it's the least distinctive of the six.

#F6F8F9 ground #FFFFFF card #1C2B33 ink #0E7C7B teal
Type
Manrope throughout — weight extremes (800 headings / 400–600 body) do the hierarchy
Structure
Card system on a soft ground; split hero with stats; contained list for publications
Effort
Medium — new tokens and card components; conventional patterns port quickly

05Display Statement

Magazine-cover energy: your name set enormous in a high-contrast serif, cream ground, a single deep blue. The most memorable first impression of the six — personality carried entirely by type, no ornament required.

  • The name is the hero — no shader, no graphic, instant identity.
  • One accent (deep blue) against cream; italics as the only "decoration."
  • Work presented as a ruled four-up strip; publications in large type with oversized italic years.
  • Great if the site should double as a speaking / writing presence, not just a directory.

06Quiet Academic

The deliberate counterweight: a single beautifully-set column, classic serif, underlined links, essentially zero chrome. The academic-homepage tradition done with real typographic care — content is the only interface.

  • Fastest possible site: no JS required, one font file, near-zero maintenance.
  • Publications become the centerpiece, set as proper citations.
  • Ages gracefully — this style has looked current for thirty years.
  • Trade-off: little room for app showcasing; tools live as an annotated link list.
Palette
#FFFFFF white #171717 ink #1D3FBF link #595959 muted
Type
EB Garamond throughout, 19px/1.65, 66ch measure
Structure
Single centered column; rules above/below nav; hanging-indent citation lists
Effort
Low — could ship in a weekend; deletes far more code than it adds

Recommendation

If one direction has to carry the whole site, Option 2 (Swiss Grid) is the strongest fit: it presents apps and publications with equal seriousness, scales cleanly as both lists grow, keeps a distinct personality with zero conceit, and reuses type assets you already ship. Option 1 is the best choice if you want the smallest visible break from today's site, and Option 3 if the apps are the main event.

Hybrids are cheap from here — e.g. Option 2's structure with Option 1's warm paper ground, or Option 5's display hero on top of Option 2's tables. Pick a base and I can fold in elements you like from the others before implementation.
