EIGHT CATEGORIES · FIFTY SCREENING TOPICS

Rubric criteria and case adaptation

These questions help the specialist agents decide what a particular case warrants. They are screening prompts, not fifty mandatory scoring items. Expand a category to read its topics, then open a topic’s details for applicability and examples of adequate or insufficient behavior.

The category structure and wording match the supplied Clinician Rubric Checklists and Case Adaptation handbook. This is a development reference, not a clinically approved assessment standard. In the integrated pipeline each criterion names its target: chart preparation, co-clinician intake/handoff, or final written assessment and plan. The patient and clinician stand-in are not scored.

1 · History and context6 topics · History & Context Elicitation

Clinician inquiry into presenting symptoms, competing differentials, organ/systemic red flags, medication/allergy/preventive/ophthalmic history, longitudinal data discrepancies, exposures, functional/access/self-management context, and collateral history.

M1-I1

Does the clinician characterize the primary symptom pattern and probe competing syndromic differentials beyond the volunteered complaint?

Applicability and what to look for

Use when: The presentation involves undifferentiated, overlapping, or self-framed symptoms where anchoring on the opening complaint risks missing a primary or coexisting etiology.

Do not require when: The diagnosis is already established and the encounter focuses solely on stable follow-up or a targeted procedural transition without new symptoms.

What satisfies it: Clinician systematically asks about symptom onset, quality, pattern, and differentiating features across plausible syndromic alternatives.

What falls short: Clinician accepts the patient's initial framing without probing differentiating symptom clusters.

Catalog support: Source support not yet settled. This describes the generic catalog item, not its applicability to a case or clinical approval.

M1-I2

Does the clinician actively screen for acute, systemic, or organ-specific red-flag symptoms during history-taking?

Applicability and what to look for

Use when: The presenting complaint or clinical trajectory carries a plausible risk of life-, organ-, vision-, or psychiatric safety-threatening complications.

Do not require when: No plausible red-flag syndrome applies to the presenting problem or visit stage.

What satisfies it: Clinician explicitly inquires about relevant warning symptoms for the affected system (including self-harm or safety risk where indicated) during assessment.

What falls short: Clinician omits red-flag inquiry and relies on the patient to volunteer alarm symptoms unprompted.

Catalog support: Source support not yet settled. This describes the generic catalog item, not its applicability to a case or clinical approval.

M1-I3

Does the clinician elicit or re-verify relevant prescription drugs, over-the-counter products, supplements, allergies, and pertinent preventive or ophthalmic history?

Applicability and what to look for

Use when: Diagnostic evaluation, safe prescribing, or preventive care depends on current medications, self-medication, administration habits, allergies, immunization/screening status, or baseline eye/vision history.

Do not require when: Medication, allergy, and relevant preventive/ophthalmic histories are already verified for the current decision and unchanged.

What satisfies it: Clinician proactively asks about relevant prescribed and non-prescribed agents, allergies, and pertinent preventive or ophthalmic history before making dependent clinical decisions, accepting unknown answers if unscripted.

What falls short: Clinician prescribes or interprets findings without checking relevant concurrent agents, allergies, or required baseline preventive/ophthalmic history.

Catalog support: Added as a design extension. This describes the generic catalog item, not its applicability to a case or clinical approval.

M1-I4

Does the clinician nonjudgmentally probe and reconcile discrepancies between objective findings and reported history or adherence?

Applicability and what to look for

Use when: Returned test results, vital signs, or symptom trajectory conflict with the patient's prior statements, self-reported diagnosis, or expected treatment response.

Do not require when: History, reported adherence, and objective findings are concordant across visits.

What satisfies it: Clinician identifies the contradiction and asks targeted questions about interval habits, pre-test actions, or prior records to clarify the clinical picture (scored on the clinician's inquiry, regardless of patient disclosure).

What falls short: Clinician ignores the discrepancy, orders invasive workup for an unverified claim, or labels treatment a failure without inquiring about confounders.

Catalog support: Source support not yet settled. This describes the generic catalog item, not its applicability to a case or clinical approval.

M1-I5

Does the clinician inquire about relevant occupational hazards, environmental triggers, travel/infectious exposures, substance use, or family history?

Applicability and what to look for

Use when: Etiology, risk stratification, or management depends on workplace irritants/ergonomics, household allergens, travel or dietary exposures, tobacco/alcohol/substance use, or hereditary disease patterns.

Do not require when: Occupational, environmental, infectious, substance, and familial factors have no bearing on the clinical problem.

What satisfies it: Clinician asks targeted questions regarding the applicable exposure, substance quantification, or family history domain.

What falls short: Clinician focuses narrowly on symptoms and omits exposure, substance, or familial risk inquiry supported by the case.

Catalog support: Drawn from a workbook example. This describes the generic catalog item, not its applicability to a case or clinical approval.

M1-I6

Does the clinician proactively inquire about functional/daily-living impact, healthcare access or self-management barriers, social support, and collateral history (with appropriate consent)?

Applicability and what to look for

Use when: Symptoms or proposed care may affect work, daily function, or independent living; logistics/access or self-management capacity may constrain care; or a witness/caregiver is involved or insight is impaired.

Do not require when: The encounter is a straightforward solo visit where functional impact, access, support, and collateral history are already clear and uninvolved.

What satisfies it: Clinician proactively asks how symptoms affect daily function/work, checks practical access/self-management capability and support (accepting unknown answers when unscripted), and in triadic visits integrates collateral history while respecting patient engagement and confidentiality/consent.

What falls short: Clinician omits functional, access, or support inquiry when relevant, accepts anosognosic minimization without exploring collateral context, or shares/solicits third-party information without addressing consent.

Catalog support: Added as a design extension. This describes the generic catalog item, not its applicability to a case or clinical approval.

2 · Examination and investigations6 topics · Examination & Diagnostic Stewardship

Targeted physical examination and vital signs, actionable exam deferral or modality coordination, clinical probability/severity scoring, confirmatory and exclusion test ordering, pre-analytical patient preparation instructions, pre-treatment safety labs, and diagnostic stewardship.

M2-I1

Does the clinician perform or arrange required targeted physical examinations and vital sign assessments, or execute an actionable exam deferral?

Applicability and what to look for

Use when: Clinical assessment requires specific bedside measurements, focused organ examination, virtual-to-in-person exam coordination, or context-sensitive deferral of an intimate or contraindicated maneuver.

Do not require when: No physical examination maneuver or modality transition is clinically relevant at the visit.

What satisfies it: Clinician performs or requests the specific examination parameters, or when deferral/modality change is needed, explains why and arranges a concrete, actionable plan to complete the assessment safely.

What falls short: Clinician omits essential physical findings, performs a contraindicated maneuver, or defers an exam without arranging an actionable follow-through plan.

Catalog support: Drawn from a workbook example. This describes the generic catalog item, not its applicability to a case or clinical approval.

M2-I2

Does the clinician assess parameters for and apply a relevant clinical probability, severity, or symptom-burden tool?

Applicability and what to look for

Use when: Case context supports structured risk stratification to guide disposition, diagnostic testing, or treatment intensity.

Do not require when: No structured clinical scoring or risk-stratification framework applies to the decision point.

What satisfies it: Clinician evaluates the constituent clinical parameters and applies the structured risk or severity assessment to inform management.

What falls short: Clinician makes disposition or testing decisions without assessing severity parameters or risk probability.

Catalog support: Drawn from a workbook example. This describes the generic catalog item, not its applicability to a case or clinical approval.

M2-I3

Does the clinician order appropriate confirmatory, secondary-cause exclusion, or staged diagnostic investigations?

Applicability and what to look for

Use when: Establishing the diagnosis, ruling out reversible mimics, or resolving equivocal baseline findings requires laboratory, microbiological, physiological, or imaging orders.

Do not require when: Diagnostic evaluation is already complete and no further diagnostic or staging investigations are indicated.

What satisfies it: Clinician orders the case-supported confirmatory and exclusion investigations at the appropriate diagnostic stage.

What falls short: Clinician initiates long-term disease-directed treatment without required diagnostic confirmation, omits secondary-cause exclusion testing, or fails to escalate staged testing when baseline results are inconclusive.

Catalog support: Source support not yet settled. This describes the generic catalog item, not its applicability to a case or clinical approval.

M2-I4

Does the clinician provide necessary pre-analytical patient preparation or specimen sequencing instructions when ordering tests?

Applicability and what to look for

Use when: Test validity depends on patient preparation prior to sampling (such as medication holds, dietary continuation/fasting, or collecting cultures before starting antimicrobials).

Do not require when: Ordered investigations require no special patient preparation or sequencing.

What satisfies it: Clinician clearly instructs the patient on required dietary, medication, or timing preparation before specimen collection or testing.

What falls short: Clinician orders a preparation-sensitive investigation without instructing the patient on pre-test requirements.

Catalog support: Source support not yet settled. This describes the generic catalog item, not its applicability to a case or clinical approval.

M2-I5

Does the clinician practice diagnostic stewardship by withholding unindicated or duplicate tests and providing an actionable deferral explanation?

Applicability and what to look for

Use when: A test is requested by the patient or commonly overused but is not clinically indicated at the current stage, or recent valid results already exist.

Do not require when: All discussed or requested investigations are clinically indicated at the current visit.

What satisfies it: Clinician refrains from ordering the unnecessary or duplicate test, explains why it is not currently helpful, and outlines actionable clinical contingencies that would prompt future testing.

What falls short: Clinician orders unindicated testing defensively or refuses a requested test abruptly without explaining the rationale and reassessment criteria.

Catalog support: Source support not yet settled. This describes the generic catalog item, not its applicability to a case or clinical approval.

M2-I6

Does the clinician order required baseline organ-function or safety-clearance investigations before starting high-risk therapy?

Applicability and what to look for

Use when: Planned pharmacotherapy or biological treatment requires pre-treatment organ function, metabolic, hematologic, infectious, or cardiac clearance.

Do not require when: The selected therapy requires no specific pre-treatment baseline laboratory or clearance testing.

What satisfies it: Clinician orders the necessary baseline safety or clearance investigations prior to or concurrently with treatment initiation.

What falls short: Clinician starts high-risk pharmacotherapy without checking required baseline safety parameters.

Catalog support: Source support not yet settled. This describes the generic catalog item, not its applicability to a case or clinical approval.

3 · Diagnostic and prognostic communication6 topics · Diagnostic & Prognostic Communication

Independently explaining the purpose of ordered investigations; interpreting returned lab, imaging, pathology, and discharge results; clarifying symptom attribution and misconceptions; communicating diagnostic uncertainty; disclosing serious news and realistic trajectories; and explaining hereditary risk.

M3-I1

Does the clinician independently explain the clinical purpose of ordered investigations and what the patient should expect, regardless of whether the patient asks?

Applicability and what to look for

Use when: The clinician orders diagnostic, staging, or baseline safety investigations during the encounter.

Do not require when: No investigations are ordered at the visit.

What satisfies it: Clinician proactively explains in plain language why each test or panel is being ordered, what question it answers, and what the procedure involves, without waiting for the patient to prompt.

What falls short: Clinician merely names or orders tests without explaining their diagnostic or safety purpose to the patient.

Catalog support: Added as a design extension. This describes the generic catalog item, not its applicability to a case or clinical approval.

M3-I2

Does the clinician interpret and explain returned laboratory, imaging, pathology, or hospital discharge findings in plain language?

Applicability and what to look for

Use when: New investigation reports or emergency/inpatient discharge summaries are available for review at the visit.

Do not require when: No new diagnostic results or discharge summaries are present at the visit.

What satisfies it: Clinician walks through relevant abnormal and reassuringly normal findings and explains how they establish the diagnosis, disease stage, or clinical stability.

What falls short: Clinician states only a diagnostic label without explaining the underlying test or discharge findings, or merely promises to review them later.

Catalog support: Source support not yet settled. This describes the generic catalog item, not its applicability to a case or clinical approval.

M3-I3

Does the clinician clarify symptom attribution, correct causal misconceptions, and provide evidence-based reassurance where serious pathology is excluded?

Applicability and what to look for

Use when: The patient misattributes symptoms to an unrelated condition or medication, or expresses fear of malignancy, organ failure, or permanent harm.

Do not require when: Symptom attribution is straightforward and no misconception or illness fear is present.

What satisfies it: Clinician directly addresses the patient's attribution belief or fear, explains the actual physiological cause of the symptoms, and reassures based on objective findings.

What falls short: Clinician ignores the patient's causal misconception or fear, leaving anxiety or false attribution unaddressed.

Catalog support: Source support not yet settled. This describes the generic catalog item, not its applicability to a case or clinical approval.

M3-I4

Does the clinician communicate diagnostic uncertainty and explain the rationale for a provisional diagnosis or staged exclusion pathway?

Applicability and what to look for

Use when: Initial findings are equivocal, normal between episodes, or discordant with clinical suspicion, requiring longitudinal observation or staged testing.

Do not require when: Available findings establish a definitive, unambiguous diagnosis.

What satisfies it: Clinician explains why current results do not yet provide final certainty and outlines the logical next diagnostic or monitoring step without premature labeling.

What falls short: Clinician asserts an unsupported definitive diagnosis on equivocal data or falsely rules out an episodic condition based on a normal baseline test.

Catalog support: Source support not yet settled. This describes the generic catalog item, not its applicability to a case or clinical approval.

M3-I5

Does the clinician disclose serious or chronic diagnoses empathetically and set realistic expectations for disease trajectory, control versus cure, or recovery timelines?

Applicability and what to look for

Use when: The patient receives a serious, progressive, or chronic diagnosis, or asks about curability, long-term prognosis, or exact recovery dates.

Do not require when: The condition is minor and self-limiting and the patient holds realistic recovery expectations.

What satisfies it: Clinician delivers diagnostic and prognostic information sensitively, clarifies ongoing disease control versus cure, and frames expected recovery or progression timelines honestly without false guarantees.

What falls short: Clinician delivers serious news bluntly, promises an unrealistic cure or fixed recovery date, or evades direct prognostic questions.

Catalog support: Drawn from a workbook example. This describes the generic catalog item, not its applicability to a case or clinical approval.

M3-I6

Does the clinician counsel the patient on hereditary transmission risk, family screening implications, or the role of genetic counseling?

Applicability and what to look for

Use when: The condition has a recognized familial or genetic component, or the patient asks about inheritance risk for relatives.

Do not require when: No hereditary condition or patient question regarding familial risk is present.

What satisfies it: Clinician accurately explains the relevant inheritance pattern or low familial risk and discusses the value of family notification or genetic counseling (while test ordering and referral execution are handled by MOD-02 and MOD-08).

What falls short: Clinician ignores family-risk questions or provides inaccurate heritability reassurance.

Catalog support: Source support not yet settled. This describes the generic catalog item, not its applicability to a case or clinical approval.

4 · Pharmacotherapy and safety7 topics · Pharmacotherapy & Safety Management

Initiating or continuing indicated therapy; resolving allergy, comorbidity, drug-interaction, and organ-function contraindications; stopping exacerbating agents; mitigating reproductive/teratogenic risk; stewarding antimicrobials and safe OTC use; and executing sequencing, titration, step-down, or adverse reaction adjustments.

M4-I1

Does the clinician initiate or continue indicated condition-directed or supportive pharmacotherapy?

Applicability and what to look for

Use when: Clinical findings or confirmed diagnoses warrant starting first-line disease-directed or symptomatic treatment, or maintaining an effective established regimen.

Do not require when: Management is exclusively non-pharmacological, or pharmacotherapy must be deferred pending diagnostic confirmation or emergency transfer.

What satisfies it: Clinician prescribes or continues an appropriate, case-supported therapeutic class for the condition (with clinically sound alternatives accepted).

What falls short: Clinician omits indicated first-line treatment or prematurely discontinues necessary maintenance therapy.

Catalog support: Drawn from a workbook example. This describes the generic catalog item, not its applicability to a case or clinical approval.

M4-I2

Does the clinician select or switch pharmacotherapy to avoid a drug-disease, drug-drug, organ-function, or allergy contraindication?

Applicability and what to look for

Use when: Standard therapy conflicts with a documented allergy, comorbidity, concurrent medication, or renal/hepatic impairment.

Do not require when: First-line standard therapy has no contraindications or interactions in the patient.

What satisfies it: Clinician recognizes the constraint, avoids or stops the contraindicated agent, and selects a safe alternative therapeutic class.

What falls short: Clinician prescribes or continues a contraindicated agent or stops needed treatment without offering a safe alternative.

Catalog support: Source support not yet settled. This describes the generic catalog item, not its applicability to a case or clinical approval.

M4-I3

Does the clinician discontinue or substitute an over-the-counter or prescribed agent that is actively causing or exacerbating the clinical problem?

Applicability and what to look for

Use when: An active prescribed, over-the-counter, or borrowed medication is driving pathology, symptom flares, or vital sign instability.

Do not require when: No current medication is exacerbating the presenting condition.

What satisfies it: Clinician explains the iatrogenic link, advises stopping and avoiding the offending agent, and provides a safer option for symptom relief if needed.

What falls short: Clinician treats the resulting symptoms while allowing the offending agent to continue.

Catalog support: Source support not yet settled. This describes the generic catalog item, not its applicability to a case or clinical approval.

M4-I4

Does the clinician address reproductive potential, preconception washout/contraception, or pregnancy safety when prescribing or reviewing therapy?

Applicability and what to look for

Use when: A patient of childbearing potential is prescribed or taking a teratogenic medication, planning conception on high-risk therapy, or currently pregnant.

Do not require when: Reproductive, preconception, or antenatal medication safety considerations do not apply.

What satisfies it: Clinician counsels on fetal/teratogenic risks, coordinates effective contraception or safe preconception medication transition/washout, avoids teratogens in pregnancy, and recommends indicated prenatal prophylaxis.

What falls short: Clinician initiates or continues teratogenic therapy without contraception/washout counseling or leaves unsafe medications unaddressed in pregnancy.

Catalog support: Source support not yet settled. This describes the generic catalog item, not its applicability to a case or clinical approval.

M4-I5

Does the clinician make an appropriate antimicrobial prescribing or withholding decision and align antimicrobial selection with clinical or microbiologic indications?

Applicability and what to look for

Use when: The current encounter involves a suspected viral or bacterial infection requiring an antimicrobial prescribing, withholding, or culture-directed adjustment decision.

Do not require when: No infectious presentation or antimicrobial therapy decision applies in the current encounter (symptomatic OTC medication safety boundaries are screened separately under M4-I7).

What satisfies it: Clinician withholds unneeded antibiotics in self-limiting viral illness with an actionable contingency plan, or selects/adjusts antimicrobial therapy to match clinical and culture susceptibility indications.

What falls short: Clinician prescribes unwarranted immediate antibiotics for a likely viral illness or continues/initiates an antimicrobial agent discordant with known susceptibilities or clinical indication.

Catalog support: Source support not yet settled. This describes the generic catalog item, not its applicability to a case or clinical approval.

M4-I6

Does the clinician execute appropriate physiological sequencing, dose titration, step-down/deprescribing, or adverse drug reaction adjustment?

Applicability and what to look for

Use when: Therapy requires staged initiation after confirmation, physiological sequencing, escalation for suboptimal control, deprescribing after remission, or modification for a treatment-emergent adverse effect.

Do not require when: Unchanged maintenance therapy is effective and well tolerated.

What satisfies it: Clinician sequences interventions safely, titrates or adds therapy when control is inadequate, steps down therapy upon sustained remission with relapse contingencies, or stops/adjusts regimens causing adverse effects.

What falls short: Clinician reverses safe physiological sequencing, leaves suboptimal control unaddressed, continues short-term therapy indefinitely without review, or ignores treatment-emergent adverse reactions.

Catalog support: Source support not yet settled. This describes the generic catalog item, not its applicability to a case or clinical approval.

M4-I7

Does the clinician counsel on safe usage boundaries, dosing limits, or overuse risks for symptomatic over-the-counter medications?

Applicability and what to look for

Use when: The current encounter involves use, patient request, or clinician recommendation of symptomatic over-the-counter analgesics, antipyretics, or decongestants carrying toxicity, rebound, or medication-overuse risks.

Do not require when: No symptomatic over-the-counter medication safety or overuse boundary applies in the current encounter (antimicrobial decisions are screened under M4-I5).

What satisfies it: Clinician advises safe dosing frequency, maximum duration limits, and explicit precautions to prevent medication-overuse headache, rebound congestion, or organ toxicity from symptomatic OTC agents.

What falls short: Clinician recommends or endorses symptomatic OTC medications carrying rebound or overuse risks without specifying safe duration or dosing boundaries.

Catalog support: Source support not yet settled. This describes the generic catalog item, not its applicability to a case or clinical approval.

5 · Shared decisions and complete explanations6 topics · Shared Decision-Making & Communication Completeness

Responding to patient concerns, questions, and delivery preferences; weighing individualized risk-benefit trade-offs and assessing decision-making capacity; counseling on conservative-to-surgical hierarchies; addressing unproven remedies and funding boundaries; negotiating care refusal with actionable deferral; and delivering complete communication across turns.

M5-I1

Does the clinician explore and respond to the patient's specific concerns, scripted questions, treatment reluctance, or delivery-route preferences?

Applicability and what to look for

Use when: The patient or caregiver raises questions or concerns, expresses hesitation about starting therapy, or prefers a specific administration route or device.

Do not require when: No specific patient question, concern, reluctance, or route preference is present.

What satisfies it: Clinician directly answers the patient's questions, validates concerns about side effects or long-term use, and accommodates safe route or device preferences (or explains clearly when a preferred option is clinically unsafe).

What falls short: Clinician ignores scripted patient questions, dismisses reluctance, or overrides safe patient preferences without explanation.

Catalog support: Drawn from a workbook example. This describes the generic catalog item, not its applicability to a case or clinical approval.

M5-I2

Does the clinician guide shared decision-making across competing clinical risks and benefits, including assessing decision-making capacity and consent when insight is impaired?

Applicability and what to look for

Use when: Initiating, extending, or stopping high-stakes therapy involves major competing risks (such as thrombosis vs. bleeding, or neuropsychiatric medication risk in frailty/dementia) or impaired patient insight.

Do not require when: Treatment follows a straightforward standard without major competing risk trade-offs or capacity concerns.

What satisfies it: Clinician clearly explains the competing clinical risks and benefits, incorporates patient/family values, and assesses decision-making capacity and informed consent when cognitive impairment or anosognosia is present.

What falls short: Clinician applies a rigid rule without discussing competing risks or initiates high-risk therapy in an impaired patient without addressing capacity and consent.

Catalog support: Source support not yet settled. This describes the generic catalog item, not its applicability to a case or clinical approval.

M5-I3

Does the clinician counsel on the hierarchy of conservative, device, minimally invasive, and surgical options?

Applicability and what to look for

Use when: The condition has staged non-surgical and procedural/surgical pathways, or requires explaining why definitive procedural intervention is indicated.

Do not require when: Management is purely medical and no procedural or surgical options are relevant or raised.

What satisfies it: Clinician outlines the appropriate progression from conservative/behavioral measures to device or surgical interventions, clarifies procedural expectations and risks, and explains when surgery is optional versus definitively indicated.

What falls short: Clinician escalates directly to invasive procedures when conservative care is first-line, or omits procedural option counseling.

Catalog support: Drawn from a workbook example. This describes the generic catalog item, not its applicability to a case or clinical approval.

M5-I4

Does the clinician provide evidence-based counseling when asked about unproven remedies, supplements, or healthcare funding/eligibility boundaries?

Applicability and what to look for

Use when: The patient asks about complementary supplements, non-recommended modalities, or publicly funded procedures for cosmetic/asymptomatic indications.

Do not require when: The patient does not inquire about unproven remedies or coverage boundaries.

What satisfies it: Clinician respectfully explains the lack of efficacy evidence or condition-specific drawbacks and clarifies clinical eligibility versus private/cosmetic options where applicable.

What falls short: Clinician endorses ineffective interventions as evidence-based or dismisses the patient's inquiry without explanation.

Catalog support: Source support not yet settled. This describes the generic catalog item, not its applicability to a case or clinical approval.

M5-I5

When a patient hesitates or declines a recommended test or treatment, does the clinician negotiate respectfully and establish an actionable deferral or interim bridging plan?

Applicability and what to look for

Use when: A patient delays, hesitates, or declines recommended diagnostic testing, medication, device therapy, or surgery.

Do not require when: The patient readily accepts the recommended diagnostic and therapeutic plan.

What satisfies it: Clinician explores the underlying barrier, explains the clinical rationale without coercion, and arranges an actionable interim safety, bridging, or reassessment plan (scored solely on clinician counseling, never on patient consent).

What falls short: Clinician pressures the patient, fails to explain the rationale for recommended care, or abandons interim safety planning when the patient declines.

Catalog support: Added as a design extension. This describes the generic catalog item, not its applicability to a case or clinical approval.

M5-I6

Does the clinician deliver complete, coherent explanations across dialogue turns rather than making unfulfilled promises to explain later?

Applicability and what to look for

Use when: The encounter requires multi-step diagnostic, therapeutic, or prognostic counseling across interactive turns.

Do not require when: No substantive clinical explanation or counseling is required at the visit.

What satisfies it: Clinician completes required explanations clearly and coherently across the conversation, ensuring that any topic introduced or promised is substantively delivered before the encounter ends.

What falls short: Clinician substitutes vague promises (e.g., 'I will explain that later' or 'read the leaflet') for required verbal counseling, or leaves key explanations truncated across turns.

Catalog support: Added as a design extension. This describes the generic catalog item, not its applicability to a case or clinical approval.

6 · Teaching and understanding6 topics · Treatment Instruction & Comprehension Verification

Actual patient teaching on medical device technique, medication administration mechanics, therapeutic onset/adherence/maintenance-vs-rescue roles, structured self-monitoring logs, pre-escalation adherence/technique verification, and meaningful comprehension checks.

M6-I1

Does the clinician actually teach or troubleshoot medical device technique rather than merely prescribing or promising instruction?

Applicability and what to look for

Use when: The management plan initiates, continues, or adjusts an inhaler, spacer, peak flow meter, positive airway pressure device, pessary, or compression garment.

Do not require when: No medical device or specialized delivery apparatus is involved.

What satisfies it: Clinician actively explains step-by-step device use, reviews technique, or troubleshoots fit/tolerance issues during the visit.

What falls short: Clinician prescribes or escalates a device without explaining how to use it, or merely promises that someone else will demonstrate it later.

Catalog support: Source support not yet settled. This describes the generic catalog item, not its applicability to a case or clinical approval.

M6-I2

Does the clinician instruct the patient on required medication timing, meal/protein coordination, posture, or supplement separation?

Applicability and what to look for

Use when: Prescribed therapy requires specific administration mechanics to ensure absorption, prevent mucosal injury, or avoid diurnal side effects.

Do not require when: Prescribed medications have no special timing, food, postural, or separation requirements.

What satisfies it: Clinician gives explicit, plain-language instructions on when and how to take the medication relative to meals, sleep, posture, or other supplements.

What falls short: Clinician prescribes a timing- or posture-sensitive medication without administration instructions.

Catalog support: Source support not yet settled. This describes the generic catalog item, not its applicability to a case or clinical approval.

M6-I3

Does the clinician explain medication mechanism, expected time to onset, full-course or long-term adherence, and maintenance-versus-rescue roles?

Applicability and what to look for

Use when: The patient starts antimicrobials, preventive/prophylactic agents with delayed onset, chronic disease controllers, or dual maintenance and reliever therapy.

Do not require when: No pharmacotherapy requiring onset, course-completion, or controller-versus-reliever education is active.

What satisfies it: Clinician explains what the medication does, when to expect clinical benefit, why completing a course or continuing chronic therapy matters after symptoms improve, and how to distinguish daily maintenance from rescue use.

What falls short: Clinician issues a prescription without explaining delayed onset, course completion, or controller versus reliever roles.

Catalog support: Source support not yet settled. This describes the generic catalog item, not its applicability to a case or clinical approval.

M6-I4

Does the clinician instruct the patient on maintaining a structured self-monitoring log and explain actionable target ranges?

Applicability and what to look for

Use when: Longitudinal diagnosis or titration depends on home physiological measurements or structured symptom/trigger diaries.

Do not require when: Structured home physiological or symptom logging is not clinically relevant.

What satisfies it: Clinician specifies what parameters to record, measurement frequency, and what target ranges or patterns should prompt review.

What falls short: Clinician advises vague self-observation without defining structured logging parameters or target expectations.

Catalog support: Drawn from a workbook example. This describes the generic catalog item, not its applicability to a case or clinical approval.

M6-I5

Before escalating dose or switching therapy for suboptimal control, does the clinician verify medication adherence, dietary implementation, and device technique?

Applicability and what to look for

Use when: A follow-up visit shows incomplete symptom or biomarker control on an initial therapeutic regimen.

Do not require when: Initial therapy is effective, or the encounter is an initial baseline assessment.

What satisfies it: Clinician explicitly checks how consistently the patient is taking the medication, following dietary measures, and using any delivery device before up-titrating or switching classes.

What falls short: Clinician escalates doses or adds second-line agents without checking adherence or administration technique.

Catalog support: Added as a design extension. This describes the generic catalog item, not its applicability to a case or clinical approval.

M6-I6

After delivering complex teaching or action plans, does the clinician perform a meaningful understanding check and address low, non-committal, or absent patient responses?

Applicability and what to look for

Use when: The clinician delivers device instruction, multi-step medication/prep rules, or complex safety-netting during the encounter.

Do not require when: No complex instruction was required, OR the prerequisite teaching/explanation was omitted by the clinician (to prevent duplicate dependent penalties).

What satisfies it: Clinician invites the patient or caregiver to summarize or demonstrate how they will carry out the plan, and if the response is minimal, confused, or absent, clarifies and re-engages supportively (scored strictly on clinician behavior).

What falls short: Clinician finishes complex instruction without checking comprehension, or ignores cues of confusion or minimal/non-committal responses.

Catalog support: Added as a design extension. This describes the generic catalog item, not its applicability to a case or clinical approval.

7 · Function, safety, prevention and support7 topics · Functional Adaptation, Public/Legal Safety & Prevention

Condition-specific non-pharmacological self-care and nutrition/bone health adapted to daily life; occupational hazard mitigation and graded return to work; driving, household, wandering, and advance care safety planning; substance cessation support; infection transmission precautions; and preventive immunizations and psychosocial/caregiver coaching.

M7-I1

Does the clinician provide condition-specific non-pharmacological self-care, dietary, bone-health, or activity strategies tailored to the patient's daily constraints?

Applicability and what to look for

Use when: Targeted dietary, postural, fluid, bone-health, physical activity, or symptom-management measures directly modify disease activity or symptom burden.

Do not require when: Non-pharmacological lifestyle measures have no specific therapeutic role in the visit.

What satisfies it: Clinician recommends actionable, condition-specific dietary, bone-protective, physical, or self-care adaptations tailored to the patient's routine and disclosed barriers.

What falls short: Clinician offers only generic wellness platitudes or ignores practical barriers in the patient's daily schedule.

M7-I2
