Revenue composition (base case)

Y1 is >80% B2C subscription revenue from derm residents and fellows, split between Core ($19/mo) for active board-prep users and Pro ($44/mo) for power users with mock exams, analytics, live tutor, and advanced simulation access. Pro mix starts at ~25% in Y1 and rises to ~40% by Y4–5 as power-user features mature and users upgrade closer to their board dates. Y2 layers in B2B2C program licenses (~17% of mix) as specialty expansion drives a step function in addressable users. Y3 is the inflection: institutional enterprise contracts (~12%), early knowledge-graph API revenue (~4%), and Sketchy-style content licensing (~3%) diversify the stack. By Y4–5, B2C is below 50% of revenue and the platform monetizes across consumer, B2B2C, institutional, and API channels.

Why the base case is the negotiation anchor

The Y3 base case ($5M ARR at 78% GM) maps to roughly the post-money valuation range a healthy Series A would support, anchoring the seed round in a return-justifying trajectory. The Y4–5 base case ($15M ARR) is the figure VCs will run their venture-multiple math against. The ambitious case is the upside scenario for term-sheet preference negotiations; the conservative case is the downside protection you can offer if pressed on revenue durability. Rising Pro mix (25→40%) is the lever that makes the same user-count trajectory produce higher ARR over time without raising headline price.

04 — Sixty-second demo

Three demos. Ninety seconds. What gets remembered.

Stage cue Open dermie.io
logged-in, fresh tab

VCs see decks every day. They see working AI-native medical software with clinical content rarely. The three demos below show calibration, content quality, and substrate — in that order, with deliberate compression and built-in contingencies for what to say if something doesn't render.

0:00–0:20

Demo 1 · The adaptive quiz cycle

Open dermie.io to a fresh quiz / dashboard

This is what a derm resident sees when they log in. The platform has already built a profile of their strengths and weaknesses across topics. As they answer, dermie captures both the answer and the confidence rating — and six weeks in, the platform knows not just what they don't know, but what they think they know that's wrong. That's the calibration we discussed.

If the dashboard doesn't load — describe the user profile concept verbally and pivot directly to a single adaptive question. Don't apologize. Confidence under live demo pressure reads as confidence in the product.
0:20–0:40

Demo 2 · The Sketchy-style mnemonic

Navigate to the visual mnemonic page; ideally pre-loaded

Now let me show you something that doesn't exist anywhere else in the category. This is a visual mnemonic in the same creative density as the leading hand-illustrated competitor — generated by our pipeline. The difference is that they need an illustrator team to produce each one. We can produce a hundred in the time it took them to produce one, and we can target each generation to the specific gaps in each learner's profile.

If image gen is slow — show a pre-generated example from the library and verbally describe the live-generation workflow. The static example still makes the point. Do not name the competitor in writing or recorded audio.
0:40–1:00

Demo 3 · The knowledge graph substrate

Open a question with grounded explanation, or a KG view if available

And here's the substrate. Every question we generate is grounded against this evidence-backed knowledge graph. When the platform tells a learner why an answer is correct, it's pulling from cited primary literature — not from the latent space of a frontier model. Static question banks can't do this. Bolt-on AI tools can't do this. This is what gets us defensible content quality at scale.

If KG visualization unavailable — open a question and walk through the citation pane on the explanation. The point is grounded explanation, not the graph view itself.
1:00–1:10

Close · Hand off to questions

Three demos, ninety seconds. Working AI-native medical software in your inbox. Happy to go deeper into any of them.

Note. Don't add a fourth demo even if asked. Let them pick which one to dive into. The economy of the ninety-second package is itself a signal of how you think about execution discipline.

The differentiation in one sentence

dermie is the only platform in the category that combines AI-native generation, multi-modal learning across a single unified learner profile, knowledge-graph-grounded content, confidence calibration, and skin-tone-inclusive imagery — with comparable visual mnemonic quality to the hand-illustrated category leader, at one-to-three orders of magnitude lower content production cost.

  • vs UWorld/Amboss: AI-native architecture, not a static bank with bolt-on features. Bolt-on AI is structurally inferior and they cannot retrofit without rebuilding.
  • vs Sketchy: AI-generated visual mnemonics produce content at one-to-three orders of magnitude lower cost than hand-illustration, with targeting to learner gaps that static libraries cannot match.
  • vs Boards & Beyond / Truelearn: They are single-modality (video, Q-bank). dermie spans Q-bank, cases, flashcards, mock exams, tutor, podcasts, and eight clinical minigames — all feeding one unified learner profile. The minigames measure skills MCQs structurally cannot reach: morphology language, prioritization, treatment sequencing, history-taking strategy.
  • vs Anki community decks: Quality-controlled, citation-grounded, board-aligned content. Anki is brilliant but unstructured; dermie is structured, evidence-backed learning.
How to use this in the room. If the VC says "how is this different from UWorld?", do not run through the matrix verbally. Pick one capability that they will immediately understand — typically calibration or knowledge-graph grounding — and explain why that single capability is structurally unavailable to a static-bank competitor.
