Chief complaint

A 52-year-old male agricultural worker presents to the dermatology clinic complaining of "a slowly growing patch on the scalp" that has been spreading over four months. He reports mild itching and notes that the centre of the patch looks like normal skin while the edge remains scaly and slightly raised. He has not tried any treatment and is otherwise well.

Step 2 · What is the most important next question?

Which of the following history items is most important to ask next?

Information revealed

The patient has been clearing brush with his teenage son. The son also developed an itchy patch on the forearm last week. The patient’s farm has cattle and feral cats. He does not recall an arthropod bite.

Educational rationale

Establishing zoonotic and environmental exposures is the single highest-yield branch at this step. Annular, scaly, centrally-clearing plaques with multiple affected household members are characteristic of tinea (often Trichophyton verrucosum in cattle handlers, Microsporum canis in cat exposures). The remaining branches are reasonable but lower yield given the clinical picture.
