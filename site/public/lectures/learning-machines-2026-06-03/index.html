Inside the learning machine

Confident.
Often wrong.

It optimizes for plausible, not true — fluent, formatted, sometimes invented, with no built-in "I don't know." You sign the note.

  1. 1Sycophancy. It leans toward agreeing with you.

Inside the learning machine

Rung 2 · It can see —
and use tools.

A word-piece — or an image — becomes tokens, then an embedding: a list of numbers capturing its meaning in context — what it is, what it's part of, even its tone. So photos, dermoscopy, and PDFs all enter in the model's native "language," and it can call tools (calculator, web, code) to act beyond its memory.

Into the clinic —
a triadic encounter.

The AMIE ambulatory study (2026): real patients, accuracy comparable to clinicians, more patient trust, zero safety stops. Now Rodman is building a clinic that is triadic from the ground up — patient, physician, and a real-time AI assistant, supervised by a second physician each encounter.

To me this is the first real step toward a better kind of care — and what I'll help develop and validate in fellowship next year. Human + machine, each catching what the other misses.

The AMIE feasibility study — with Google DeepMind.

The hidden risk

Lean on it, and
the skill fades.

GPS weakened our sense of direction. Offload the reasoning to an assistant and our clinical skills can atrophy the same way. Give expert endoscopists an AI polyp-finder, then take it away — their own detection rate drops.

Budzyń et al., Lancet Gastroenterol Hepatol 2025. If seasoned experts erode, what about trainees still building the gestalt?

Appendix · our debt

Skin of color
is our blind spot.

Models learn from the images we gave them — and our archives badly underrepresent dark skin. SOTA AI (and dermatologists) underperform on darker skin and rare disease. And AI-generated derm images are realistic but biased and often wrong (JEADV 2025).

Daneshjou's DDI dataset proved it — and showed diverse data closes the gap. The fix is ours.

the data debt
public derm datasets:
Fitzpatrick V–VI barely present
→ models worse on dark skin
→ dermatologists, too
→ fix: diverse data + fine-tuning

Appendix · for Q&A

Who's accountable
when it's wrong?

  1. 1FDA. Regulates clinical AI as a device (SaMD) — hundreds cleared. The hard problem: "locked" vs "adaptive" models that keep learning.
  2. 2EU AI Act. Medical AI is "high-risk" — strict documentation and human oversight.
  3. 3Liability today. The clinician who signs the note. The model is not a defendant.
  4. 4Still catching up. Validation standards, reimbursement, post-market monitoring.
